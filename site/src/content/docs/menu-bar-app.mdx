Sonocles.app has no window and no Dock icon. It lives in the menu bar and
opens as a popover, and it is a thin shell over the same `Service` the CLI
drives and the HTTP API controls. That is deliberate: anything clever added
to the popover would be behaviour the measurements never saw and the API
cannot reach, so there is nothing clever in it.

## The mark

The mark is the state. Dimmed, it is idle; at full strength, it is
listening. It is a template image, so it takes the menu bar's colour in
light and dark and the dimming survives as opacity.

The sockets are up either way. Idle means capture is off, not that the app
is unreachable — `POST /start` works while the mark is dim, and that is the
point of the split.

## The popover

**The meter.** Twenty cells over the useful range, −60 dBFS to clipping, and
a numeric peak beside them. The number is driven straight from the audio
thread, independent of the model, which is what makes it useful: a quiet
room sits at the bottom of the bar but still moves the number, and that is
the proof that the microphone is live when nobody is speaking. `––` means
no reading at all.

**The words.** The current utterance, up to three lines, head-truncated so
the newest word is always visible. Height is reserved, so an arriving word
never shoves the rest of the popover down — at five frames a second that
would be a twitch, not an interface.

**Two numbers.** `lag` is how far behind the live audio the last frame was;
`every` is the gap since the frame before it. An unmeasured value shows as
`··`, never as `0`. Rendering absence as a number is how an earlier build
spent a session insisting it was real-time while measuring nothing at all.

**Engine.** Parakeet 160 ms (the default), 320 ms, 1280 ms, or Apple
SpeechAnalyzer on macOS 26. Switching restarts capture: the models differ,
and a half-swapped pipeline would report numbers belonging to neither.
[Engines](/docs/engines) has the measurements that chose the default.

**Endpoints.** `HTTP :7357` and `WS :7358`. Labels, not switches — the
sockets bind at launch and stay up for the life of the app.

**Control API.** The token, masked to its ends, with Show, Copy and Rotate,
and the path of the file it lives in. Rotate is two clicks: the first arms
it for six seconds, the second does it. A rotation cuts off every other
paired client until it re-reads the file, so it is not something to do on
one slip.

**Start listening / Stop.** The same thing as `POST /start` and
`POST /stop`. Disabled while the models are being prepared.

**Quit.** Takes the sockets down with it. There is no daemon behind the
app; the app is the process.

## The first run

Before the first listen, the popover shows what the engine is doing instead
of the meter: *Finding model files*, then a progress bar for the download,
then a pulse for compiling. The bar is real when there is a fraction to
report and a pulse when there is not — compiling has no measurable progress,
and animating a bar to look busy would be the same lie as reporting an
unmeasured latency as zero.

<Callout kind="note" title="One time only">
The models cache on disk. Every launch after the first goes straight to the
microphone prompt, or straight to listening once that is granted.
</Callout>

## What it costs to leave running

Idle, nothing: two bound ports and a dimmed mark. Listening, the Neural
Engine and a microphone tap, and no CPU your encoder is competing for. That
is the entire reason the thing was written, and it is what makes it
something to leave listening for the length of a take.
